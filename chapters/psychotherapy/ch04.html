Case Conceptualization

BIG IDEACase conceptualization is the counselor's theory-based map of the client's experience and inner world; it is one of the hardest skills to learn.
Theory is the map of the client's inner world.
Case conceptualizationthe technical term for the counseling art of viewing(Gehart, 2016, p. 17)
DON'T CONFUSE · "ASSESSMENT" = 2 TASKS
Theory-informed assessment= case conceptualization
vs
Diagnostic assessment= clinical assessment
ELEMENTS OF CASE CONCEPTUALIZATION
IIntroduction of Client
salient demographics: gender, age, ethnicity, occupation or school, family status, sexual orientation; client and significant others
PPresenting Concern
how all parties define the problem; referral, attempted solutions, others' perspectives
BBackground Information
recent changes, precipitating events; trauma, substance abuse, mental disorders, previous counseling
SStrengths and Diversity
personal, relational, spiritual; each diversity factor = resources + limitations
TTheoretical Case Conceptualization(s)
initial understanding of personal and relational dynamics
Problem-generating system
The set of relationships that generated the idea that there is a problem; each person may have a different definition.
PSYCHODYNAMIC · DEFENSE MECHANISMS
Psychodynamic defense mechanismsautomatic psychological processes that a person uses to ward off anxiety and external stressors(Gehart, 2016, p. 26)
Acting outinner conflict dealt with through action rather than reflecting on feelings
Denialrefusing to acknowledge a painful reality apparent to others
Displacementfeelings transferred to a less threatening substitute
Help-rejecting complainingcomplaining but rejecting all help offered
Humorfocusing on the amusing or ironic side of a conflict
Passive aggressiona façade of cooperation that covertly resists or undermines
Projectionown unacceptable feelings attributed to another, unaware
Projective identificationprojection, but aware and justifying them as reactions to the other, who then acts out the projection
Rationalizationreassuring but incorrect explanations that conceal true motives
Reaction formationacting diametrically opposed to the denied feeling
Repressionunacceptable desires removed from awareness; unaware of the conflict
Splittingcannot see self or others as both positive and negative
Sublimationunacceptable impulses channeled into socially acceptable activities
Suppressionintentional avoidance of difficult thoughts and feelings

Clinical Assessment and Diagnosis

BIG IDEAClinical assessment identifies the oases and obstacles: symptoms, diagnosis, safety, and needed resources.
Find the oases and obstacles.
Clinical assessmentfocuses on the psychiatric and medical symptoms of clients(Gehart, 2016, p. 59)
COMPETENCIES
mental status examDSM-5 diagnosismonitoring client safetymedical conditions outside the counselor's scopecase management
PURPOSES OF DIAGNOSIS
coordinate care with other professionskeep clients and the public safedetermine referralsidentify courses of treatment
COUNSELING MODELS AND DIAGNOSIS
HumanisticUnnecessary and possibly a detriment (Rogers). Reductionistic, artificial, ignores the relationship, involves judgment, intellectualizes suffering.
Family systemsBehavior makes sense in context; one person labeled for a role in a relational web. Don Jackson's myth of normality.
PostmodernLabels shape identity, silence strengths; self-fulfilling prophecy, telescopic vision. Diagnosis = one of several voices.

Treatment Plan

BIG IDEAA treatment plan addresses the problems identified in conceptualization and assessment, using a theory that fits the client and evidence-based practice.
Chart a course.

SECTION 5 OF 6 · LEAVE A TRAIL

Progress Notes

BIG IDEAProgress notes are the counselor's legal record of professional care; keep them factual and minimal.
Factual and minimal.
Progress notesare counselors' primary means of showing that they are rendering professional care that conforms to legal and ethical standards(Gehart, 2016, p. 111)
✓ Includecase number · date, time, length · who attended · progress and symptoms · crisis assessment · interventions and response · plan · signature (not initials) with license
✗ Avoidclient names · highly subjective observations · third-party details
Crisis exceptiondocument safety assessment, safety plan, and actions taken in detail
PROGRESS NOTESPSYCHOTHERAPY NOTES
NatureThe official medical fileThe counselor's case conceptualization file
AccessShared with professionals and payers; clients usually have access; can be subpoenaedHigher HIPAA protection; property of the counselor; clients have no right to them
ContentSymptoms, interventions, client responseImpressions, hypotheses, conceptualization
RulesDetailed payer requirementsNo requirement to keep; must be kept separate
FORMATS
FORMATPARTS
SOAPSubjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan designed for physical conditions
DAPData, Assessment, Plan P sometimes = Progress
BIRPBehavior, Intervention, Response, Plan
LOGISTICS
sessions 25 or 50 min (groups 60–90)CPT codes for billingdocument consultations and collateral contacts (release on file)locked file cabinetdigital: HIPAA-level security
Progress notesofficial, accessible to clients
vs
Psychotherapy notesprivate, separate, more protected
MEMORY HOOKBIRP = "Behavior → Intervention → Response → Plan": what happened, what I did, how they reacted, what's next.

Evaluating Progress in Counseling

BIG IDEAMeasure progress early and regularly, with tools brief enough that clients will actually complete them.
Measure early and regularly.
OPTIONPROSCONS
Nonstandardized (client and counselor reports)Low cost; every session; adaptable to diverse clientsLess reliable and valid; harder with children and severe pathology
Standardized (tested measures)More reliable and valid; compare across clients and timeCost; time; not always normed for diverse groups or languages
REAL-WORLD TIPS
1
First session
2
5 min or less
3
Regular intervals
4
Before the session
5
Frame it as helpful
ASSESSMENT IN A DIVERSE SOCIETY
norm groups may not fit cultural subgroupsacculturationexpectations of impersonal interactionsclient's preferred language
MEASURES
Outcome Rating Scale (ORS)Ultra-brief; 4 visual analog scales, under 1 minute: overall, individually, interpersonally, socially. Child and young child (faces) versions.
Session Rating Scale (SRS)Ultra-brief rating of the session: relationship, goals/topics, approach/method, overall. Child and young child versions.
Outcome Questionnaire (OQ-45.2)45 items, about 5 minutes; symptom distress, interpersonal relationships, social role performance. Youth versions (YOQ, YOQ-SR).
SCL-90-R and BSISCL-90-R: 90 items, 12–15 minutes. BSI: 53 items, 8–10 minutes. Ages 13+; 9 symptom subscales; global indices (GSI, PSDI, PST).
BASIS-24 and BASIS-3224 or 32 items rated 0–4 on difficulty in areas of functioning.
Specific measuresE.g., BDI (depression), BAI (anxiety).
ORShow the client is doing (outcome)
vs
SRShow the session went (alliance)
