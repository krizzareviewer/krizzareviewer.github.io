Counseling and Psychotherapy

Jones-Smith (2021)Prochaska & Norcross (2010)
BIG IDEACounseling and psychotherapy overlap, but differ in who they help, how deep they go, and the kind of change they aim for.
Counseling · Prevent + Presenta process concerned with helping normally functioning or healthy people to achieve their goals or to function more appropriately(Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 4)
Psychotherapy · Repair + Pastreconstructive, remedial, in-depth work with individuals who suffer from mental disorders or who evidence serious coping deficiencies(Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 4)
SIDE BY SIDE
ASPECTCOUNSELINGPSYCHOTHERAPY
FocusEducational, situational, developmental; the present and conscious awarenessUncovering the past; insight into chronic problems
Type of changePreventive mental healthReparative change
GoalsDevelopmental and educationalRemedial
LengthRelatively briefLonger-term and more intense

They are overlapping areas of professional competence (Jones-Smith, 2021).

ADVICE-GIVING

A quick fix that fosters dependence.

Jones-Smith (2021). See Issue #12, Declining to Give Advice (Chapter 2).

Psychotherapy (working definition)Psychotherapy is the informed and intentional application of clinical methods and interpersonal stances derived from established psychological principles for the purpose of assisting people to modify their behaviors, cognitions, emotions, and/or other personal characteristics in directions that the participants deem desirable.(Norcross, 1990, in Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, pp. 3–4)
THIS DEFINITION IS
  • Theory-neutral
  • Clinical methods and interpersonal stances on equal footing
  • Requires mutually agreed-upon goals (otherwise therapy becomes subtle coercion)
MEMORY HOOKCounseling = Prevent + Present Psychotherapy = Repair + Past

The Role of Theory

Jones-Smith (2021)Prochaska & Norcross (2010)Capuzzi & Stauffer (2022)
BIG IDEAA theory is the therapist's road map; without one the therapist is "winging it."
Theorya set of statements one uses to explain data for a given issue(Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 1)
Psychotherapy theory (system)a consistent perspective on human behavior, psychopathology, and the mechanisms of therapeutic change(Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, p. 4)
A THEORY DECIDES
1
What the client's problem is
2
What can correct it
3
How the relationship will be used

It carries a view of the nature of people (good or evil?), which decides which capacities get developed. (Jones-Smith, 2021)

EACH THEORY CHANGES PEOPLE THROUGH
insightbehavioremotioncatharsiscognition

(Capuzzi & Stauffer, 2022)

TRUE OR FALSE
✗A theory of personality is required ✗Adopting a theory is a lifetime vow

Behavioral, constructionist, and integrative therapies may lack a theory of personality. (Prochaska & Norcross, 2010)

THERAPIES MULTIPLIED
36 (1959)
over 400 today

Many claim success without controlled research. (Prochaska & Norcross, 2010)

CRITERIA FOR EVALUATING THEORIES · Jones-Smith (2021)
CClarity
Clearly outlines its basic assumptions.
CInternally consistent
Its parts do not contradict one another.
CComprehensive
Explains as many events as possible; precise, parsimonious, with testable hypotheses.
HHeuristic
Promotes further research.

Overview of the Theory Chapters

Corey (2024)
BIG IDEACorey groups his 11 approaches into 4 families; Jones-Smith (2021) calls them forces and adds a 5th. This map is the backbone of the reviewer.
PleaseCallEverySingleNeighbor
1ST FORCE · INSIGHTPsychodynamic Approaches
Psychoanalytic therapySigmund FreudUnconscious factors motivate behavior; the first six years determine later personality.
Adlerian therapyAlfred Adler; Rudolf Dreikurs (popularized it in the U.S.)Growth model: responsibility, creating one's own destiny, meaning and goals.
2ND FORCE · ACTIONCognitive Behavioral Approaches
Behavior therapyB. F. Skinner, Albert Bandura, Marsha LinehanApplies the principles of learning to specific behavioral problems.
Cognitive behavior therapyAlbert Ellis (REBT); A. T. Beck (cognitive therapy); Judith Beck; Christine Padesky (strengths-based CBT); Donald MeichenbaumREBT: highly didactic, cognitive, action-oriented. Cognitive therapy: primary role to thinking.
Choice theory/Reality therapyWilliam Glasser; Robert WubboldingShort-term; client assumes responsibility in the present.
3RD FORCE · EMOTIONExperiential and Relationship-Oriented
Existential therapyViktor Frankl, Rollo May, Irvin YalomChoice, freedom and responsibility, self-determination; a philosophy of counseling, not a unified school.
Person-centered therapyCarl Rogers; Natalie Rogers1940s, nondirective reaction against psychoanalysis; the relationship is the prime determinant of outcome.
Gestalt therapyFritz and Laura Perls; Miriam and Erving PolsterAwareness and integration of body and mind; here-and-now; therapist takes an active role.
4TH FORCE · CONTEXTSystems and Postmodern
Feminist therapyJean Baker Miller, Carolyn Zerbe Enns, Lillian Comas-Diaz, Thelma Bryant-Davis, Laura BrownPsychological oppression of women and their sociopolitical status.
Postmodern approachesde Shazer and Berg (solution-focused brief therapy); White and Epston (narrative)No single truth; reality is socially constructed; clients are the experts in their own life.
Family systems therapyMurray Bowen, Virginia SatirChange the individual by working with the family.
5TH FORCE (JONES-SMITH, 2021)Neuroscience
Neuroscience, interpersonal neurobiology, trauma-informed counselingJones-Smith Ch. 20Brain science in counseling; see Chapter 22.
INTEGRATIVEIntegrative Psychotherapy
Integrative psychotherapyCorey Ch. 15; Jones-Smith Ch. 21; Prochaska & Norcross Ch. 15–17Combining theories and techniques; see Chapter 23.
WHY PSYCHOANALYSIS COMES FIRST
Later systems are extensions, modifications, or reactions against it. Adlerian therapy is analytic but does not focus on unconscious dynamics.
A COMPLETE THERAPY
Experiential therapies are, in a sense, emotion-focused; cognitive behavioral therapies are the action-oriented therapies. A complete therapy addresses thinking, feeling, and doing.
DON'T CONFUSE
Corey's book lists experiential chapters before cognitive behavioral ones, but cognitive behavioral is the 2nd Force. Motivational interviewing is "postmodern" in Corey but 3rd Force in Jones-Smith.
MEMORY HOOK"Please Call Every Single Neighbor" = Psychodynamic, Cognitive behavioral, Experiential, Systems, Neuroscience. Routes to change: insight, action, emotion, context.

Common Factors

Prochaska & Norcross (2010)Jones-Smith (2021)
BIG IDEADifferent therapies share core ingredients that explain their similar outcomes; the strongest is the relationship.

ROLE OF THE RELATIONSHIP
1

2

Improvement from receiving special attention. Studies therefore need an attention-placebo control (Paul, 1967).

The unique techniques of each therapy. Corey covers the common factors approach to integration in Ch. 15.

The Helping Relationship

Capuzzi & Stauffer (2022)
BIG IDEAThe relationship is the vehicle for reaching goals, not the goal itself. It needs stages, core conditions, and strategies.
Helping relationshipin which at least one of the parties has the intent of promoting the growth, development, maturity, improved functioning, and improved coping with life of the other(Rogers, 1961, in Capuzzi & Stauffer, 2022, p. 5)

HELPING STRATEGIES · Rapport → Data → Depth
ABuild rapport (active listening)
Attending and encouragingPosture, eye contact, and words show the client is heard.
Restating and paraphrasingRestating = "repeating the exact words used by the client." Paraphrasing = "repeating the thoughts and feelings of the client but in the words of the counselor or therapist" (p. 14).
Reflecting content and feelingFeeds back the ideas (content) or the emotions behind them (feeling).
Clarifying and perception checkingClarifying = asks the client to explain their words. Perception checking = asks the client to confirm or correct the counselor's perceptions.
SummarizingHighlights what is significant and sets priorities.
BGather data
QuestioningOpen questions cannot be answered yes/no.
Probing and leadingProbing = digs into one area. Leading = steers to a topic.

Mechanisms of Change

Prochaska & Norcross (2010)
BIG IDEATheories meet on a small set of change processes. Process = how people change; content = what needs to change.
Processes of changethe covert and overt activities that people use to alter emotions, thoughts, behaviors, or relationships related to a particular problem or more general patterns of living(Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, p. 11)
TRANSTHEORETICAL = "ACROSS THEORIES"
Global theories
Change processes (middle level)
Specific techniques

Expert therapists plan at the middle level.

THE 5 C'S · EXPERIENTIAL = INSIDE · ENVIRONMENTAL = OUTSIDE
PROCESSEXPERIENTIAL LEVELENVIRONMENTAL LEVEL
AWARENESS OR INSIGHT THERAPIES
Consciousness raisingFreud: "to make the unconscious conscious"Feedback (about oneself)Education (about the environment)
Catharsisa hydraulic modelCorrective emotional experiencesDramatic relief
Choosingstressed by existential therapistsSelf-liberationSocial liberation
ACTION OR BEHAVIORAL THERAPIES
Conditional stimuliCounterconditioning (change one's response)Stimulus control (restructure the environment)
Contingency controlSkinner: behavior is controlled by its consequencesReevaluation (change one's view of consequences)Contingency management (change the consequences)
CounterconditioningCounterconditioning is learning to do the healthy opposite(Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, p. 15)
THERAPEUTIC CONTENT · LEVELS OF CONFLICT
1
Intrapersonal: anxieties and defenses; self-esteem; personal responsibility
2
Interpersonal: intimacy and sexuality; communication; hostility; control
3
Individuo-social: adjustment versus transcendence; impulse control
4
Beyond conflict to fulfillment: meaning in life; the ideal person
MEMORY HOOK5 C's: 3 for awareness (Consciousness raising, Catharsis, Choosing) + 2 for action (Conditional stimuli, Contingency control). Experiential = inside; environmental = outside. Content: Me → Us → Society → Meaning.
