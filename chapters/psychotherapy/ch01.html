Counseling and Psychotherapy

Big idea. Overlapping fields: they differ in who they help, how deep they go, and the change.
Counseling or psychotherapy? Which one do I need?
COUNSELING
"a process concerned with helping normally functioning or healthy people to achieve their goals or to function more appropriately" (Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 4)
PSYCHOTHERAPY
"reconstructive, remedial, in-depth work with individuals who suffer from mental disorders or who evidence serious coping deficiencies" (Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 4)

Psychotherapy (working definition) "the informed and intentional application of clinical methods and interpersonal stances derived from established psychological principles for the purpose of assisting people to modify their behaviors, cognitions, emotions, and/or other personal characteristics in directions that the participants deem desirable" (Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, pp. 3–4; Norcross, 1990)
  • Theory-neutral; clinical methods and interpersonal stances on equal footing; requires mutually agreed-upon goals (otherwise therapy becomes subtle coercion).
  • Counseling and psychotherapy are overlapping areas of professional competence (Jones-Smith).

The Role of Theory

Big idea. A theory is your road map; without one you are "winging it."
A theory tells you the problem, the cure, and how to use the relationship.
Theory "a set of statements one uses to explain data for a given issue" (Jones-Smith, 2021, p. 1)
Psychotherapy theory (system) "a consistent perspective on human behavior, psychopathology, and the mechanisms of therapeutic change" (Prochaska & Norcross, 2010, p. 4)
A GOOD MAP (THEORY) HAS
✓
Clarity clearly outlines its basic assumptions
✓
Internally Consistent parts do not contradict one another
✓
Comprehensive explains as many events as possible; precise, parsimonious, testable
✓
Heuristic promotes further research
  • A theory decides what the client's problem is, what can correct it, and how the relationship will be used (Jones-Smith). It holds a view of the nature of people (good or evil?), which decides which capacities get developed.
  • Each theory changes people through a mechanism: insight, behavior, emotion, catharsis, or cognition (Capuzzi & Stauffer).
  • A theory of personality is not required (behavioral, constructionist, and integrative therapies may lack one); adopting a theory is not a lifetime vow.
  • Therapies multiplied from 36 (1959) to over 400, many claiming success without controlled research (Prochaska & Norcross).
