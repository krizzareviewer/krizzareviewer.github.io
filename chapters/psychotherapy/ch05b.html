Empathy and mirroring (Kohut)Empathy at the forefront; mirroring confirms the client's sense of self
Holding environmentStructure, consistency, and routine that build ego strength (object relations)
Mutual enactments and self-disclosureRelational therapists: mutual enactments, not knowing, careful, timely self-disclosure
Intersubjective respondingUse the relationship itself for insight; explore the client's interpretation of it (culture, gender, class, education, childhood) nondefensively, with curiosity

Transactional analysis (Berne)Find the crossed response style. Parent (rules and norms), child (emotional memory), adult (integrates); conflicts come from unhealthy interaction among the three
Transference-focused psychotherapyEvidence-based for narcissistic personality disorder; interprets devalued and idealized images of self and others

JUNGIAN TECHNIQUEWHAT IT IS
Word Association Test100 neutral and emotional words; first thought. Slow reaction time, perseveration, slips, stammering, or refusal signal complexes
Dream analysisJung's primary route to the unconscious; a series of dreams, recurring themes; subjective (the client's life) or objective (archetypes); record on waking. Dreams are prospective and compensatory.
Transference and countertransferenceIncludes archetypal projections from the collective unconscious; personal and archetypal elements analyzed
Rorschach Inkblot TestProjective: what is seen in symmetrical inkblots; inspired by Jung's introversion and extraversion; scored by location, properties, content
Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)Henry Murray with Christina Morgan (1935); 20 ambiguous pictures (16 in the children's edition); the client tells a story; clinical judgment
Psychological types and MBTI2 attitudes (introversion, extraversion) × 4 functions (thinking, feeling, sensing, intuiting) = 8 types. Sensing and intuiting irrational; thinking and feeling rational. MBTI: 16 preferences
Play therapy and serial drawingArt, play, drama, dance, music, visualization; children draw each visit and project the unconscious; self-healing archetype
SandplayA world built in the sand; nonverbal and indirect; trauma, bereavement; helps prevent retraumatization

Multicultural Perspective

IN ONE LINEUseful across cultures if culture is considered, but criticized as individual-focused.
The unconscious and attachment matter for everyone.

STRENGTHS

  • Useful for diverse populations if culture is considered; the unconscious and attachment matter for everyone
  • Ego psychologists are relevant to ethnic minority clients; Erikson shows how social and cultural factors influence people across life
  • Mitchell adds a cultural dimension: no objective psychic truths
  • Relational and intersubjective approaches use the client's interpersonal context and unconscious cultural organizing principles

SHORTCOMINGS

  • Criticized as irrelevant to anyone outside a verbal middle-class background
  • Feminist and multicultural critics: focuses on the individual rather than family or environment
  • Does not address social justice and external sources of problems

Contributions and Limitations

IN ONE LINEResearch supports its efficacy; brief forms do not fit severe disorders.
Research supports the efficacy of psychodynamic therapy.

CONTRIBUTIONS

  • Shows how current behavior repeats patterns from early development
  • Object relations helps with separation and individuation, intimacy, dependence vs independence, identity
  • Analysts are more directive, limit sessions, make interpersonal problems central, build a strong working alliance
  • Alliance quality is critical to outcomes; research supports its efficacy; brief work is as effective as CBT and other mainstream approaches

LIMITATIONS

  • Object relations criticized for blaming mothers and leaving out fathers
  • Brief psychodynamic therapy generally not suited to severe characterological disorders or severe depression
  • Long-term analysis remains costly and time consuming
