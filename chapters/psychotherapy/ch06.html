Real Adlerians Inspire Reorientation.

a weaving that leads to a tapestry.

The phases are not linear.

RealRRelationship
AdleriansAAssessment
InspireIInsight

THE FOUR PHASES · COREY NAME / CAPUZZI NAME
Encouragement in every phase
1 Relationshipcollaborative; focus on the person, not the problem; point out strengths
2 Assessmentlifestyle assessment: subjective and objective interviews
3 Insighttentative hunches: "I could be wrong, but I am wondering if…"
4 Reorientationnew goals, act "as if," catch themselves; change happens between sessions

Corey's full names: Establishing the Relationship · Assessing the Individual's Psychological Dynamics · Encouraging Self-Understanding and Insight · Adaptive Reorientation and Reeducation. Capuzzi: Relationship, Investigation, Interpretation, Reorientation.

Gehart's psychoeducation by phase
Relationship: the client's role and rights, how Adlerian counseling works, what social interest is. Assessment: why assess, what lifestyle and private logic are. Insight: information that counters inaccurate private logic and explains style of life. Reorientation: commitment and tasks turn insight into action.
ORDERS OF CHANGE

Lifestyle Assessment

IN ONE LINERead the style of life through two interviews, the family, and early recollections.
How would your life be different?
KEY POINTS · HOW THE ASSESSMENT FLOWS
Subjective interviewthe client's life story, with empathy and genuine interest
The Questionusually ends the subjective interview
Objective interviewfacts: onset, history, coping, lifestyle
Lifestyle summarystrengths and mistakes, teacher-to-student; client may agree, disagree, or resist
The QuestionHow would your life be different, and what would you be doing differently, if you did not have this symptom or problem?(Corey, 2024, p. 129)
Early recollectionsstories of events that a person says occurred [one time] before he or she was 10 years of age(Mosak & Di Pietro, 2006, p. 1, in Corey, 2024, p. 130)
PARTWHAT IT COVERS
Objective interview(a) how problems began (b) precipitating events (c) medical history (d) social history (e) why therapy now (f) coping with life tasks (g) the lifestyle assessment
Family constellationFamily atmosphere, birth order, parental relationship and values, extended family and culture; siblings' gender, an absent parent, which child was favored
Early recollectionsDo not have to be accurate; show the present view of self, others, and life. At least three memories to see a pattern.
Life tasks assessedWork · communal life and friendship · love relationships · self-acceptance · spirituality · parenting
Organ inferiorityPhysical weak points, or the belief of being physically inferior, that shape the style of life
Lifestyle analysis partsFamily atmosphere and values, early recollections, encouragement
TYPE OF PERSONSLOGAN

TECHNIQUEWHAT IT DOES
EOffering encouragementPrinciple and technique: the therapist expresses faith and belief in the client; especially with children. Corey: a fundamental attitude more than a technique; builds courage when people see strengths, feel they belong, and have hope.
QAsking "The Question""If I could magically eliminate your symptom immediately, what would be different in your life?" Solution-focused therapists relabeled it the "miracle question." Gehart's wording: "What would be different in your life if you didn't have this problem"
AActing "as if"Act for a short time as if one had the quality one lacks; try on new behaviors and roles
PPush-button techniquePicture a pleasant, then an unpleasant experience; control feelings by controlling thoughts. Mosak used it with depression; a real button can be given to a child
CCatching oneselfCatch oneself just before reverting to an old habit; substitute new behavior
MMidas techniqueExaggerates the client's irrational demands until they look absurd (King Midas)
PPleasing someoneDo something nice for someone else to build social interest
MORE TECHNIQUESWHAT IT DOES
Creating imagesDescribe the client with an image ("the complainer"); images such as Bozo the Clown help clients laugh at their style
BrainstormingAfter mistakes are identified, client and counselor generate alternative beliefs
Paradoxical intentionEncourage the client to do what they complain about, to overcome their opposition
Psychoeducation and task settingInformation in every phase, then specific tasks based on it
Interpretation of symptomsPhase 3. Purpose focused (not labels), tentative, open to revision, strengths emphasis; finds the purpose of the symptom in the style of life (unlike psychoanalytic interpretation)
Self-concept statementsInferiority-based (falls short of self-ideal), inadequacy-based (falls short of cultural values), guilt-based (falls short of moral beliefs)
Challenging and reframing basic mistakesDirectly challenge ("What would happen if everyone had that belief?") or gently reframe ("positive reframing")
Natural and logical consequencesDreikurs, mainly for children. Natural = happen without human action; logical = arranged when natural ones are not enough. Preferred to reward and punishment
AntisuggestionParadoxical: discourage change or encourage the symptom (e.g., scheduling worry time) so the client gains control
Spitting in the soupMake the covert benefits of a symptom overt so it leaves a "bad taste"
Reflecting "as if"Reflective questions → list "as if" behaviors from easiest to hardest → start with the easier ones, with encouragement
Adlerian dream analysisClues to the style of life; a rehearsal of future action, not infantile wishes; the dreamer decides the meaning; forgotten dreams may mean postponing action
BibliotherapySelf-help reading; the six E's: Educate, Encourage, Empower, Enlighten, Engage, Enhance
ConfrontationPart of encouragement: holds mistaken goals and beliefs up like a mirror, then reorientation toward what works
Socratic questioningQuestions that lead to insight and cognitive change; uncover private logic and goals; short- and long-term consequences
Adlerian Play Therapy (AdPT)Developed by Kottman for children with emotional, behavioral, or academic problems
Others named by Corey
Immediacy, advice, humor, silence, externalization, reauthoring, avoiding the traps, confrontation, stories and fables, early recollection analysis, lifestyle assessment, Metta meditation, task setting and commitment, homework, terminating and summarizing. Adlerians are pragmatic and focus on motivation modification more than behavior change.

Applications

IN ONE LINEA growth model, not a medical model, so it fits many settings.
A growth model, not a medical model.
KEY POINTS · A GROWTH MODEL FITS MANY SETTINGS
child guidanceparent–child counselingcouples and family therapygroup counselingindividual counselingcorrectional and rehabilitation counselingmental health institutions
FORMAT · FLEXIBLE, USUALLY SHORT TERM
individual, family, groupparent and couples workshops, social clubs
SETTINGHOW IT IS USED
FamilyFamily atmosphere, constellation, and each member's goals; Adlerians pioneered open-forum family counseling, starting with Adler's child guidance centers in the 1920s
GroupAdler and coworkers used groups in Vienna as early as 1921; Dreikurs extended and popularized it; problems are mainly social
SchoolUnderstand students in their totality; ask the purpose of misbehavior (wanting to count, belong, connect, or lacking courage); encouragement; empathy
Multiple-therapist approachDreikurs: reduces transference and countertransference problems
Serious mental health issuesAdler preferred "discouraged" to "pathological"; neuroses come from exaggerated inferiority or too little community feeling

Multicultural Perspective

IN ONE LINEThe person in a social context; less fitting for clients who do not want to explore the past.
The person in a social context.

STRENGTHS

  • Well suited to diverse populations and social justice work
  • Focuses on the person in a social context; assessment goes beyond the individual to family, community, society
  • Values cooperation over competition
  • Lifestyle assessments can add sociocultural factors and diversity contexts

SHORTCOMINGS

  • Less effective for clients who do not want to explore childhood, early memories, family, or dreams, or who do not see the point of a lifestyle analysis
  • Some clients expect the counselor to be the expert
  • Some cultures see revealing family information as inappropriate

Contributions and Limitations

IN ONE LINEHugely influential, but its ideas are vague and hard to research.
The "grandfather of modern psychotherapy."

CONTRIBUTIONS

  • Flexible and integrative: "theoretically consistent and technically eclectic"
  • Suited to brief, time-limited therapy; early recollections can shorten therapy
  • Many therapists acknowledged their debt; called the "grandfather of modern psychotherapy"

LIMITATIONS

  • Many ideas are vague and general, which makes research difficult
  • Writings more poetic than scientific; not scholarly in the modern sense
  • So many concepts adopted by other models: is it viable as a stand-alone approach?
